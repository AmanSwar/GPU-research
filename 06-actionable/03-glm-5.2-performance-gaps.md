## 1. Executive summary: the five moves that matter

1. **Repair the evidence base (3 measurements, ~2 days).** (a) True-C1 nsys
   re-capture (§0). (b) FP8-checkpoint A/B **at both C1 and C10** — the trace
   scout found an operating-point trap: FP8 halves dense bytes but *doubles*
   NVFP4 expert bytes, and at batch 10 expert streaming is ~35 GB/cycle at
   ~70% of the byte floor, so FP8 plausibly wins at C1 and loses at C10/C64
   (§3.G6). (c) The fixed-accept step-time bake-off (01 item 6), with sampling
   mode pinned — scout 2 found our temp-0.6 verify carries ~150–200 µs/cycle
   of sampling work a greedy published number would not (§3.G9).
2. **Unblock draft depth 5-1-6** with `--speculative-moe-runner-backend
   triton` (worst-case cost of the triton draft MoE is now *measured-bounded*
   at ~1–2% of cycle vs a +2–7% depth gain), and race it against the newly
   found `deep_gemm` + `SGLANG_NVFP4_CKPT_FP8_NEXTN_MOE=1` variant that both
   sidesteps the IMA cubin *and* halves draft weight bytes (§3.G4, §3.G5).
   Expected: **+2–7% at C1** (correction #2 unchanged).
3. **The C10 verify package** — the 10-parallel arm is where the trace
   actually points, and it is a leaderboard arm: force one-shot fused
   allreduce at ≤128 tokens (+3–5%), extend the fused_a/router JIT kernels
   past their M≤16/M≤4 gates (+4–7%), shallower/adaptive draft at C10
   (+3–8%), and the spec-glue patch series — metadata grids, sampling,
   plan-stream (+2–4% together). Composed conservatively: **159.3 → ~180–195
   tok/s/stream** (§4.2).
4. **Hybrid precision through the existing loader hook** — the
   `NVFP4_CKPT_FP8_ATTN_QUANT_MODULES` list already quantizes BF16 checkpoint
   tensors to FP8 at load; extending it to `fused_qkv_a_proj_with_mqa` and
   `o_proj` is a two-file change covering ~51% of non-expert decode bytes. Do
   it **only if** move 1(b) says bytes matter at C1; ceiling ~8% per
   correction #4, up to ~15% if the A/B surprises (§3.G7).
5. **Cost plane: attack prefill-stalls-decode at C64.** Ledger candidate B is
   closed — the 10.5% un-graphed AllReduce is entirely prefill-phase, and the
   C64 steady idle's addressable half (~5%) sits in front of eager,
   launch-bound prefill kernels (prefill CUDA graph is disabled for MLA).
   Levers: launch batching/graphlets for prefill, chunk-size tuning, adaptive
   spec tiers, and TBO as the standing port project (§5.3).

**Composed expectation, shown working (§4):** C1 headline 349.5 →
**~365–400** from catalogued levers alone (depth ×1.02–1.07, glue ×1.00–1.03,
precision ×1.00–1.08 gated). **The 500+ frontier is not reachable from this
catalog** — it requires localizing the 1.4× forward-pass gap (move 1c) and
re-validating the SM-packing thesis on a real C1 trace. That conclusion
sharpens 01: the biggest number is still the unattributed forward-pass gap,
and we now know the trace we thought explained it cannot.

### 2.2 The C1 step — 8.92 ms, decomposed by arithmetic only

`[verified]` bench decomposition (survives §0): full forward 6.91 ms; marginal
draft forward 0.64 ms; 3-1-4 cycle 8.92 ms = verify 6.9 + 3 drafts ≈ 1.9 +
glue. What we now know about its *interior* comes from one captured (never
replayed) bs=1 graph — graphId 68, 1,947 kernels `[verified, capture-time
inventory]`:

- The engine dispatches a **different, leaner kernel set at bs=1**: JIT
  `fused_a_gemm` qkv (9.65 µs) and q_b (4.8 µs) ×78, dedicated
  `router_gemm_kernel` ×75, **one-shot** fused AR ×155 (not the twoshot+norm

  Dispatch keys on batch size (`decode_cuda_graph_runner.py:556`).
- Consequence: **every per-kernel "C1" cost model in the corpus was built
  from C10 kernels that do not run at C1.** The TGV/cuBLAS-router findings

The open questions that only the true-C1 re-capture answers: the kernel-time

### G2. Force one-shot fused allreduce for ≤128-token batches (C10 lever)

- **Mechanism:** at bs=10 (40 verify tokens) every one of the 155 fused-AR
  sites runs the mnnvl **twoshot** path — `twoshotAllreduceKernel` (7.59 µs)
  + separate `rmsNormLamport` (7.79 µs) = 15.4 µs/pair, 2.37 ms/replay —
  because flashinfer's AUTO heuristic flips at ~10.6 tokens (1 MiB:
  `MNNVL_ONE_SHOT_THRESHOLD = 64*1024*8*2`,
  `.venv/.../flashinfer/comm/trtllm_mnnvl_ar.py:39-51`) and SGLang never
  passes `use_oneshot` (`layers/layernorm.py:214-221`,
  `layers/flashinfer_comm_fusion.py:772`). TRT-LLM's own guidance — quoted at
  `layers/communicator.py:160-162` — says one-shot up to 128 tokens for
  min-latency. At bs=1 the same sites run a single `oneshotAllreduceFusion`
  kernel (7.69 µs), so this is free at C1 and pure upside at C10.
- **Evidence:** graph 44: 155 twoshot + 155 lamport per replay; graph 68: 155
  single oneshot; draft graph 113 at exactly 10 tokens sits under the
  threshold `[verified, SQL]`.
- **Fix (code, small):** in `flashinfer_allreduce_fusion`'s call site
  (`flashinfer_comm_fusion.py:766`) pass `use_oneshot=True` when
  `input_tensor.shape[0] <= 128`, else `None`. Workspace already sufficient
  (twoshot@2048 buffer = 50.3 MB ≥ oneshot@128 = 12.6 MB, and
  `is_buffer_size_sufficient` checks per-strategy). Graphs bake the choice at
  capture — the bs 3–32 buckets re-capture with the new path automatically on
  restart.
- **Effect:** replaces 15.4 µs pairs with one ~8–10 µs kernel at 155 sites ≈
  0.9 ms of a 16.8 ms cycle = **+3–5% at C10**; ~0% at C1. Removing one
  Lamport wait boundary per site may also absorb skew better than raw kernel
  time suggests. Amdahl bound: fused-AR is ~14% of cycle wall.
- **Effort:** port/code (one function). **Risk:** numerics unchanged (same
  reduction, different schedule); a mid-size regression is possible if the
  one-shot curve bends above 64 tokens — measure at bs 16/32 buckets too.
- **Confirm/kill:** kernel histogram — twoshot count must drop to 0 in the
  bs 3–32 verify/draft-extend graphs; then TPOT at C10 with repeats. Kill if
  one-shot at 40 tokens ≥ 15 µs (microbench first: measured points are 7.7 µs
  @4 tok, 9.5 µs @10 tok — the 40–128 token region is extrapolated).
- **Confidence:** `[verified]` mechanism; `[inferred]` magnitude.
- **vs 01:** new — no 01 entry saw this; it partially *explains* 01 §0.3's
  collective share at the profiled point.

### G3. Extend the small-M specialized GEMMs past their M gates (C10 lever)

- **Mechanism:** the two most expensive per-layer dense GEMMs at C10 fall off
  their specialized kernels on hard M thresholds. (a) qkv_a: the JIT
  `fused_a_gemm` requires `1 <= M <= 16`
  (`sglang/kernels/ops/gemm/fused_a_gemm.py:54`); verify M=40 → falls back to
  TGV bf16 (`TgvGemmCuteExtKernel_cta64x16x128`, 16.16 µs ×78 = 1.26
  ms/step). (b) router: `dsv3_router_gemm` supports M 1–16
  (`dsv3_router_gemm.py:5`) and `MoEGate` additionally caps at M≤4 on SM100
  (`deepseek_v2.py:524`) → cuBLAS `nvjet_tss_64x8_splitK` (9.77 µs) +
  fp32 `splitKreduce` (3.02 µs) ×75 = 0.97 ms/step. The draft graphs (M=10)
  *do* use fused_a — proving the threshold, not the weight, is the mechanism.
- **Evidence:** trace graph 44 kernel inventory + the two gate lines
  `[verified]`. Combined ~13% of C10-step GPU time (lane-discounted: ~9–10%
  of wall).
- **Fix (new kernel, bounded):** extend the M range of both JIT kernels to
  M≤64 (templates already parameterize the M tile) and raise the SM100
  MoEGate cap with a re-verification bench. Per repo rules this is
  jit-kernel territory (`add-jit-kernel` skill).
- **Effect:** if the specialized kernels scale (fused_a 9.65 µs at M=1 vs TGV
  16.16; router kernel ~2–3 µs vs 12.8), **+4–7% at C10**. **Zero at C1**
  (thresholds already satisfied). Below the 4% floor per-item — judge by
  kernel histogram + repeats.
- **Effort:** new kernel. **Risk:** the JIT kernels were tuned for M≤16; at
  M=40 they may lose to TGV/cuBLAS. ncu the candidates at M=40 **before**
  writing anything (Loop K step 1).
- **Confirm/kill:** TgvGemm and the nvjet+splitKreduce pair must disappear
  from the verify-graph histogram, replaced by
  `fused_a_gemm_kernel`/`router_gemm_kernel`; per-kernel time must beat the
  incumbents at M=40 in ncu.
- **Confidence:** `[verified]` mechanism; `[unverified]` that the extension
  wins at M=40.
- **vs 01:** sharpens item 4's "dense GEMM" slice into two named, gated
  kernels — and relocates the work from C1 (where 01 aimed it) to C10.

### G5. The FP8-nextn variant: halve draft weight bytes AND dodge the IMA cubin

- **Mechanism:** the in-tree escape hatch for exactly our case (NVFP4
  checkpoint, BF16 nextn MoE) — `enable_nextn_moe_bf16_cast_to_fp8`
  (`deepseek_common/utils.py:105-113`) — is doubly gated off: env
  `SGLANG_NVFP4_CKPT_FP8_NEXTN_MOE` defaults False (`environ.py:643`) AND it
  requires the active MoE runner to be `deep_gemm`, evaluated under
  `speculative_moe_backend_context` during draft-worker build
  (`eagle_worker_v2.py:154-164`, `moe/utils.py:517-531`). The MTP layer is
  19.91 GB BF16 (~2.5 GB/rank); draft MoE BF16 weight streaming measured 406
  µs/cycle at C10; the marginal C1 draft forward is 0.64 ms of which weight
  streaming is over half (~0.33 ms at 7.67 TB/s).
- **Fix (research → flag):** launch with `--speculative-moe-runner-backend
  deep_gemm` + `SGLANG_NVFP4_CKPT_FP8_NEXTN_MOE=1`. Two unknowns to clear
  first: does deep_gemm function on CUDA/sm100 with `moe_a2a_backend=none`
  and TP-sharded experts (the HIP resolution insists on deepep + ep_size>1,
  `overrides.py:1685-1699`)? and does the loader-time gate read the draft
  context's runner? One server load answers both.
- **Effect:** ~200 µs/cycle at C10 (~1.2%); ~5–7% of the C1 cycle if the
  weight-stream half of the 0.64 ms marginal draft halves across 3 drafts
  (scout 1's estimate; treat as optimistic `[inferred]`). Secondary value: an
  independent falsifier for the IMA mechanism — if depth 5 survives on
  deep_gemm too, the dynB cubin is confirmed as the culprit. If it works it
  supersedes G4's triton fix (triton likely slows the draft; this speeds it).
- **Effort:** flag flip if the gates resolve; small code otherwise.
  **Risk:** accuracy of FP8-cast draft weights affects only accept length,
  never correctness (verification is exactness-preserving) — watch accept.
- **Confirm/kill:** server loads; draft-phase dynB cubins replaced by
  deep_gemm kernels; accept unchanged ±0.1; draft-phase GPU time falls.
- **Confidence:** `[verified]` gates; `[inferred]` viability on sm100.
- **vs 01:** new — 01 never found this hatch; it upgrades item 2 from "fix a
  crash" to "fix the crash and make the draft cheaper".

### G12. Shared-experts fusion — the scouts disagree; resolved to a small, cheap A/B

- **The disagreement, honestly:** the trace scout measured the shared-expert
  BF16 splitK group at 1.86 ms/verify (12.4% of verify wall, 10× its byte
  floor) and projected **up to +9%** from `--enforce-shared-experts-fusion`.

  nothing** at decode.

  kernel time that the 28% lane-overlap (§2.1) already hides. The honest
  expected value is **0–3%**: whatever alt-lane criticality exists, plus 300
  launches/pass removed, plus the shared expert's bytes moving from BF16 to
  NVFP4 inside the grouped GEMM (a genuine byte win on the byte-bound MoE
  island), minus one extra expert slot of grouped-GEMM work on the main lane.
- **State:** `disable_shared_experts_fusion=True` is forced by the model
  override for `moe_runner_backend='flashinfer_trtllm'`
  (`overrides.py:2290-2294`, logged in our runs). *Why* it is forced —

  unestablished (§7.4).

- **Effect:** 0–3% at C10; ~0 at C1 (fewer tokens, smaller shared cost).

- **Confidence:** `[verified]` both measurements; `[inferred]` net effect.
- **vs 01:** 01 had no entry; correction to the scout report: the trace
  scout's +9% headline is **rejected as stated**, replaced by 0–3%.

## 8. Addendum: four corrections from the roofline work (2026-08-17, later)

Written after an independent pass that measured this box's ceilings and drove the
MoE and dense GEMMs directly. Three of these change a verdict in this document;
one closes an open question for free. Same labelling rules.

### 8.1 The MoE is **not** closed — and §2.1 and `01` item 11 contradict each other

§2.1 records the expert GEMMs at "~70% of its 4.6 ms floor … **Confirms ledger
#11: closed**". But `01-ranked-opportunities.md` item 11 reads *"MoE
down-projection pipeline under-fill — `K=256` per rank is exactly one `tileK` at
TP8"* and ranks it live. **This document closed what its predecessor opened**,
and the aggregate is the reason: 70% is the mean of a healthy kernel and a
broken one.

ncu on the two grouped GEMMs, driven at the deployed shape
(`ep_size=1, tp_size=8` → 257 local experts × intermediate 256), 192 tokens
`[verified, benchmark/runs/roofline/moe-tp8.csv]`:

| kernel | bytes read | analytic min | achieved | **% of peak** | time |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `bmm_E2m1_E2m1E2m1` (w13) | 433.6 MiB | 434 MiB | 6,351 GB/s | **82.8%** | 72.2 µs |
| **`bmm_Bfloat16_E2m1E2m1` (w2)** | 216.3 MiB | 217 MiB | **3,892 GB/s** | **50.8%** | **62.4 µs** |

**Byte efficiency is perfect** — measured reads match the analytic minimum to
0.4%, so there is no wasted traffic. The entire loss is tile shape: w2's
reduction dimension is `intermediate_size_per_partition = 2048/8 = 256`
(`fused_moe_triton/layer.py:279`) against a kernel tiled `t128x32x512`
`[verified]`. The microbenchmark's 62.4 µs reproduces the C64 trace's 63.6 µs,
so this is the real kernel, not a synthetic proxy.

**The decisive experiment has been run, and it removes the cheap route.**
`ep_size=8` restores K=2048 and measures **62.4 → 41.7 µs, a 1.50× on the same
bytes** `[verified, ncu]`. End to end it **lost**: −2.4% output throughput
(2,226 → 2,173 tok/s), +3.5% median TPOT, C64/3-1-4/sharegpt, identical flags,
`ep_size=8` confirmed live in `/get_server_info`, `moe_a2a_backend=none` so no
DeepEP a2a was pulled in `[verified, runs/moe-ab/]`. Server start also doubled,
150 s → 300 s.

Leading explanation `[inferred]`: TP-MoE is load-balanced *by construction* —
`02-measurement-plan.md` §0.2 makes exactly this point, that under `ep_size=1`
every rank computes a slice of every selected expert so routing imbalance cannot
produce rank imbalance. EP removes that guarantee, and a step ends when the
slowest rank ends, so a 1.5× faster GEMM on a rank that then waits is worth
nothing.

**Net effect on the catalog:** ledger #11 stays reopened, `01` item 11 is
promoted from "+2–5%, speculative" to **"1.5× on the kernel, measured, reachable
only by a kernel fix"**, and the config shortcut is closed with a recorded
negative. The kernel fix is a `tileK=256` variant of the trtllm-gen cubin, or
persuading the selector to pick the existing `t128x8x256` tile the microbenchmark
chose at this shape — worth an ncu comparison before any code.

### 8.2 The floor model needs mechanism floors, and they bound G2 and G3

Every floor in §2.1 is a byte or FLOP floor. Two *mechanism* floors were measured
on this box and neither is derivable from bytes `[verified,
benchmark/kernels/ceilings.py]`:

| floor | measured | how |
|---|---:|---|
| **NCCL, payload-free** | **22 µs** | a 128 KiB all-gather costs 22.1 µs; a 2 MiB one costs 26.9 µs |
| **cuBLAS GEMM, trivial** | **~9 µs** | every dense projection at decode shapes lands 9–13 µs regardless of M, N, K |

Consequences for two entries here:

- **G2 is strengthened.** "~15× NVLink wire time … pure sync latency" is right,
  and now has a number: at ~2 MiB the wire contributes ~3.6 µs against a 22 µs
  mechanism floor. One-shot at ~8–10 µs is close to the floor, so the +3–5%
  estimate is well-founded rather than optimistic.
- **G3 is bounded.** This document's own figure — `fused_a` at 9.65 µs at M=1 —
  is already *on* the launch floor. Extending it to M≤64 can beat TGV's 16.16 µs
  but cannot go far below 9 µs, so the ceiling on that kernel is ~1.7×, not more.
  Worth doing; not worth over-promising.

Also measured, and relevant to G2's open question #9: NCCL is **non-monotonic**
on this fabric. All-reduce costs 190.6 µs at 8 MiB but 110.6 µs at 16 MiB, and
130.5 µs at 2 MiB but 63.3 µs at 4 MiB `[verified, 8-rank sweep]`. Any size-gated
heuristic should be checked against the measured curve, not assumed smooth.

### 8.3 Pin the HBM denominator: 7,666 GB/s is ncu's peak, ~7.0–7.3 TB/s is achieved

`02-measurement-plan.md` §1 cites 6.98–7.28 TB/s sustained read; ncu's own
sustained-peak denominator on this box is **7,666 GB/s**, cross-checked three
ways (kernels independently measured at 6,351 / 6,340 / 5,745 GB/s reported by
ncu as 82.8% / 82.7% / 75.0% of peak — all implying 7,666 to within 0.03%)
`[verified]`. Both numbers are correct and they are not interchangeable: a
"% of peak" quoted from ncu divides by 7,666, a "fraction of achievable" divides
by ~7.1 TB/s. §2.1's "4.6 ms floor" should say which it used — it is a ~10% swing
on every MoE headroom claim in this document.

Related trap, since it nearly caused a wrong verdict here: a torch reduction
reaches only 5,649 GB/s on this box. Using torch as the yardstick flatters every
kernel by ~26%.

### 8.4 Open question #11 is closed: the C64 capture is correctly labelled

Audited the same four ways §0 used `[verified]`. `runs/sweep-capacity/server.log`
shows `#running-req` peaking at **8**, which is the *per-DP-rank* count —
`dp_size=8`, so 8 × 8 = 64 concurrent, as labelled. Confirmed by shape

**M1 therefore only needs the C1 arm.** The C64 half can be skipped.
